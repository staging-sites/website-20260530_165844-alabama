## Early Reports and the 1947 UFO Wave

The first recorded UFO reports in Alaska emerged in the summer of 1947, at the height of the post‑war “flying saucer” craze that swept the United States following pilot Kenneth Arnold’s famous sighting in Washington state. On 9 July 1947, three teenagers in Anchorage claimed to see a white, disc‑shaped object streaking rapidly across the sky near [Elmendorf]({{ 'elmendorf/' | relative_url }}) Air Force Base. They reported speeds far beyond typical aircraft of the time, and because they were familiar with military planes, their description drew local attention. However, no independent confirmation by military observers was documented, and authorities did not issue an official explanation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.adn.com/alaska-life/2022/08/28/a-strange-light-bouncing-up-and-down-in-the-sky-the-history-of-ufo-sightings-in-alaska/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: adn.com">[Anchorage Daily News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">adn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Anchorage Daily NewsA ‘strange light bouncing up and down in the sky’: The history of UFO sightings in Alaska - Anchorage Daily News...</span></span></span>

## Military and Aviation‑Linked Incidents

### Elmendorf AFB, May 1950

Among the earliest military‑related reports in Alaska was an incident at Elmendorf Air Force Base on 5 May 1950. Several U.S. Air Force personnel from the 625th Aircraft Control and Warning Squadron described an extremely fast, luminous object moving erratically over the base. According to secondary accounts, a formal report of “unusual occurrence” was filed and classified, but the Air Force did not offer a definitive public explanation and later records remain sparse. Such incidents were typical of the Cold War era, when isolated radar anomalies and unexplained visual sightings were often filed but rarely investigated publicly.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.discoveryuk.com/mysteries/mystery-at-the-edge-of-the-map-alaska-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: discoveryuk.com">[Discovery UK]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">discoveryuk.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Discovery UKMystery at the Edge of the Map: Alaska UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Discovery UKMay 13, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2026</span></span></span>

### Japan Air Lines Flight 1628 (1986)

One of Alaska’s most widely quoted and thoroughly documented UFO encounters occurred on the night of 17 November 1986. Japan Air Lines Cargo Flight 1628, a Boeing 747‑200F, was cruising over eastern Alaska when Captain Kenju Terauchi, an experienced aviator, reported seeing unusual lights pacing the aircraft. According to reports, the crew observed three distinct objects visually and on radar for roughly 50 minutes. The incident attracted international attention because the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and military radar centres confirmed radar returns coinciding with the sighting, and the FAA initially indicated an official investigation. Despite extensive media coverage, no conclusive identification was ever released, and sceptics have suggested conventional explanations such as rare atmospheric or radar artefacts, instrument glitches, or optical illusions.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.ufoevidence.org/cases/case287.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoevidence.org">[UFO Evidence]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoevidence.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO EvidenceJapan Air Lines Flight 1628 Over Alaska - Alaska, United States - November 17, 1986 - UFO EvidenceNovember 17, 1986...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 17, 1986</span></span></span>

### National Guard and Radar Reports (1987)

In March 1987, National Guard personnel in several western Alaska locations reported a brightly lit object racing through the night sky. Military spokespeople offered tentative explanations such as satellites entering the atmosphere or high‑altitude AWACS radar aircraft, but tracking data lacked corroboration, and no definitive identification was achieved.
