## Introduction

Belleville’s place in Wisconsin UFO history comes from a short burst of sightings in early 1987 rather than a decades-old local legend. What made the episode unusual was that the first widely reported witness was a police officer, Glen Kazmar, and that other law-enforcement personnel soon reported seeing similar lights. The combination of official witnesses, repeated reports, media attention and later investigation transformed a brief winter flap into one of the state’s most durable UFO stories. Nearly four decades later, the case survives less as an unresolved mystery than as a piece of civic identity, remembered through Belleville’s annual UFO Day celebrations and the town’s continuing reputation as one of Wisconsin’s self-described UFO capitals. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://bellevillewi.com/ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bellevillewi.com">[bellevillewi.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bellevillewi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span> <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ufodays.org/events/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufodays.org">[2ufodays.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufodays.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AboutUFO Days is an annual event held in Belleville, Wisconsin commemorating a UFO sighting that made national news in 1987.Read more...</span></span></span>

## Glen Kazmar's January 1987 sighting window

The Belleville flap began on the night of 15 January 1987. According to later newspaper accounts and investigator summaries, Belleville police officer Glen Kazmar reported seeing a formation of bright lights while on patrol near the village. The lights were described as red, white and blue, with a particularly bright blue light at the centre. Kazmar reportedly observed the display around 8:30 pm and again shortly before 3:00 am. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_0115_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 0115 dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sighting 1987 in Belleville, Wisconsin15 Jan 1987 — Is Kazmar, in general, a believer in UFOs? &quot;I keep an open mind on... What gave the report unusual weight was that Kazmar was not presented as a casual observer staring into the night sky for entertainment. He was on duty, using the habits of observation expected from a patrol officer. Local reports soon added that Dane County and Green County officers had also observed unusual lights and that dispatchers sought information from aviation authorities. One widely circulated claim held that an FAA facility in Aurora, Illinois, indicated a radar target in the area at roughly the same time, although later retellings often varied in detail and did not produce publicly available radar records that conclusively resolved the matter. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://ufodays.net/2019/08/13/the-other-ufo-days-belleville-wi/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufodays.net">[UFO Days and UFO Stories]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufodays.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the other ufo days belleville wi</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Other UFO Days-Belleville, WI13 Aug 2019 — Category: Elmwood, Recent Posts, UFO Days Event, UFO HistoryTags: Belleville, George Wheel...</span></span></span>

The original descriptions were not especially exotic by UFO-history standards. Witnesses generally spoke about lights rather than a clearly visible structured craft. That distinction matters. Many UFO cases become harder to evaluate because observers see distant lights at night without reliable information about altitude, distance or size. Belleville was no exception. The case gained attention because multiple observers reported something unusual, not because investigators recovered physical evidence or obtained clear photographs. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_bell_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 bell dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO center probes abduction reports from Belleville, New Glarus areas.&quot;, &quot;UFOs seen again...Read more...</span></span></span>

## How a single report became a wider flap

The January sighting did not remain an isolated incident. During the following weeks, newspapers around southern Wisconsin carried additional reports of strange lights. Residents in and around Belleville and nearby New Glarus reported seeing unusual aerial objects, and local discussion quickly expanded beyond the original police observation. Headlines from the period show how the story evolved from one officer's report into a regional wave of sightings. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_0115_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 0115 dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sighting 1987 in Belleville, Wisconsin15 Jan 1987 — Is Kazmar, in general, a believer in UFOs? &quot;I keep an open mind on... UFO sighti...</span></span></span>

This widening phase is important because it illustrates how UFO flaps often develop. Once a highly publicised report appears, more witnesses come forward. Some may have seen the same event from different locations. Others may reinterpret earlier observations through the lens of the new story. The result can be a genuine increase in reports without necessarily proving that every report concerns the same object or phenomenon.

Belleville attracted attention from organised UFO researchers, including investigators associated with the J. Allen Hynek Center for UFO Studies. Don Schmitt, who would later become nationally known for his work on Roswell-related claims, became one of the most visible investigators connected to the case. The National Center for UFO Studies announced that it had begun examining the reports, treating the concentration of sightings seriously enough to warrant field investigation. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_bell_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 bell dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO center probes abduction reports from Belleville, New Glarus areas.&quot;, &quot;UFOs seen again...Read more...</span></span></span>

Several features made the flap attractive to investigators:

* Reports from law-enforcement personnel.
* Claims that sightings occurred from different locations.

## What investigators thought they had found

By spring 1987, the Belleville flap had become large enough to draw public meetings and regional media attention. One frequently repeated account describes hundreds of people gathering at Belleville High School to hear investigators discuss their findings. Don Schmitt and colleague Richard Heiden presented conclusions that treated at least some of the reports as genuine unidentified objects rather than obvious aircraft or astronomical misidentifications. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wisconsinfrights.com/wisconsin-ufo-festivals/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wisconsinfrights.com">[Wisconsin Frights]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wisconsinfrights.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wisconsin Frights UFO Capital of Wisconsin?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Explore these 3 UFO Hotspots30 Jun 2021 —... For believers, the case seemed stronger than many UFO stories because it involved officers, dispatch communications and repeated reports from separate witnesses. The phrase "Belleville Wave" later became attached to the cluster, reflecting the idea that the town had experienced a concentrated period of unusual activity rather than a single incident. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.wisn.com/article/ufo-sightings-in-wisconsin/39013138" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wisn.com">[WISN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wisn.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings in wisconsin</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Have UFOs been spotted in Wisconsin?8 Feb 2022 — Author and longtime UFO report investigator Donald Schmitt said the Belleville Wave...</span></span></span>

Sceptics, however, pointed to the same limitations found in many flap cases. Witnesses observed lights at night under conditions where distance and scale are difficult to judge. Publicity can encourage additional reports that are not necessarily connected to the original event. Claims about radar contacts and official confirmation often become stronger in retelling than they appear in contemporary documentation. The available record does not provide a definitive explanation, but neither does it provide evidence strong enough to establish an extraordinary one. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_bell_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 bell dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO center probes abduction reports from Belleville, New Glarus areas.&quot;, &quot;UFOs seen again...Read more...</span></span></span>

That ambiguity is one reason the Belleville flap remains part of Wisconsin UFO history. It was never cleanly solved, yet it also never produced the sort of evidence that would move it beyond the category of an intriguing but unresolved local mystery.

## Why Belleville remembered this case when many towns did not

Most UFO flaps fade quickly. Belleville's survived because the story attached itself to local identity at exactly the moment when the village received regional and national attention. News crews arrived, investigators held meetings, and residents found themselves living in a place suddenly associated with something strange. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://apnews.com/general-news-48bd7339f9ef4c3aaa367fb0046bdbb8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News Wisconsin community remembers strange lights with UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AP NewsWisconsin community remembers strange lights with UFO...October 20, 2018 — 19 Oct 2018 — Since the late 1980s, Wolf&#x27;s homemade UF...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 20, 2018</span></span></span>

The police connection mattered. A report from an officer carried more public credibility than a rumour passed around a café or tavern. Whether or not the officers correctly interpreted what they saw, their involvement made the incident feel like a public event rather than a private anecdote. That distinction mirrors a broader pattern seen elsewhere in Wisconsin UFO folklore, including Elmwood, where police testimony also helped sustain local memory. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufowisconsin.com/county/reports/r1987_bell_dane.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufowisconsin.com">[ufowisconsin.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufowisconsin.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">r1987 bell dane</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO center probes abduction reports from Belleville, New Glarus areas.&quot;, &quot;UFOs seen again...Read more...</span></span></span>

Another reason the case endured is that it arrived during a period when UFOs still attracted significant local newspaper coverage. Residents could collect clippings, attend talks and debate competing explanations. The story became woven into community memory before the internet fragmented attention into countless other topics.
