## Introduction

The St. Clair Triangle case became famous partly because it unfolded over active police radio channels rather than through later retellings. In the early hours of 5 January 2000, dispatchers and officers across several southern [Illinois]({{ 'illinois/' | relative_url }}) towns were discussing an unusual aerial object while it was apparently still moving through the area. Those recordings gave investigators something rare in UFO history: a partially time-stamped, multi-jurisdiction sequence that could be compared against witness statements, geography and later reconstructions. The tapes do not prove that an extraordinary craft crossed Illinois, but they do establish that multiple officers were reacting in real time to something they considered unusual. ## What the Police Radio Tapes Really Show

The core value of the dispatch recordings is not dramatic language or explicit claims of extraterrestrial craft. In fact, much of the traffic sounds cautious, procedural and uncertain. Officers repeatedly try to work out whether they are looking at an aircraft, a star, or something explainable before describing it as unusual. That matters because the tapes preserve reactions before the event became nationally known. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://skeptoid.com/episodes/435" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skeptoid.com">[Skeptoid]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skeptoid.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SkeptoidThe St. Clair Triangle UFO7 Oct 2014 — So now let&#x27;s turn to the radio transcripts, beginning with the original dispatch: DISPATCH...</span></span></span>

The chain began after Highland resident Melvern Noll reported a large illuminated object to police. Dispatch then relayed the information to nearby departments. Lebanon officer Ed Barton initially treated the report sceptically, reportedly joking about whether the caller had been drinking, but soon radioed that he himself was seeing a bright light east of town. One of the best-known dispatch excerpts came around 4:18 a.m., when Barton reported that the object “doesn't look like an aircraft”. A few minutes later, at roughly 4:21 a.m., he asked dispatch to contact Scott Air Force Base to determine whether anything military was operating in the area. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://files.secure.website/wscfus/10582237/26186165/triangular-ufos-an-estimate-of-the-situation-by-david-marler-john-b-alexander-richard-m-dolan-mark-rodeghier-sam-maranto-george-wingfield-omar-fowler-team-enki-free-pdf.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: files.secure.website">[WebStarts]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">files.secure.website</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WebStartsTriangular UFOs: An Estimate of the SituationOn the St. Clair County dispatch tapes at 4:21 am, Officer Ed Barton of. ## How the Sighting Moved Across Police Jurisdictions

The radio traffic suggests a rough east-to-west or north-east-to-south-west progression across the Metro East area of Illinois. Later investigators reconstructed the route using dispatch times, officer locations and local road geography. ### Highland and the first alert

The initial report came from Highland shortly after 4:00 a.m. Noll described a large, low object with bright lights that appeared far bigger than a normal aircraft. Highland police did not simply log the call and ignore it. ### Lebanon and Summerfield

Officer Ed Barton in Lebanon became the pivotal witness because he moved from scepticism to active observation. His transmissions place the object somewhere east of town, near Summerfield. Barton described intense white lights and a changing appearance inconsistent, in his view, with an ordinary aircraft. ### Shiloh, Millstadt and Dupo

## What the Dispatch Record Fixes and Leaves Uncertain

The recordings are often treated as if they settle the case. They do not. What they really provide is a framework separating stronger evidence from weaker embellishment.
