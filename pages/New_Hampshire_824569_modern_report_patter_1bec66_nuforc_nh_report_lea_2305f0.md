## Introduction

[New Hampshire]({{ 'why-new-hampshire-became-ufo-country/' | relative_url }})’s NUFORC reports are useful because they show what people claimed to see, where they saw it, and when they decided it was unusual enough to report. They are much less useful as direct proof that genuinely unexplained objects were present. The National UFO Reporting Center (NUFORC) database contains more than 1,200 New Hampshire entries and remains one of the largest public collections of UFO witness reports in the state. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/subndx/?id=lNH" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports for State NHNUFORC Reports for State NH.; Open, 03/18/2025 11:15, Loudon; Open, 03/08/2025 19:10, Londonderry; Open, 02...</span></span></span>

## How NUFORC records New Hampshire sightings

A typical New Hampshire entry includes the alleged event date, town, shape category, a witness narrative and the date on which the report was submitted. Recent entries may also include an assessment field indicating whether investigators believe the object was probably identifiable. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/ndx/?id=loc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NUFOR C Reports by Location USA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports by LocationUSA - New Hampshire, 1276. ## Why self-reported entries need corroboration

The central limitation of the New Hampshire reports is that most begin as self-submitted accounts. NUFORC itself describes the database as a large collection of witness reports and notes that reports received before March 2023 had not yet been graded under its newer evaluation system. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=20008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 20008NUFORC UFO Sighting 20008. A witness can be sincere and still misidentify what they observed. Distance, darkness, lack of reference points and unfamiliar aircraft lighting all complicate night-time observation. Modern UAP research efforts, including NASA's independent study, have repeatedly stressed that eyewitness reports alone rarely provide enough information for firm conclusions. Better assessments usually require supporting sensor data, photographs, radar records, flight information or independent witnesses. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team Report... A report may describe an enormous object hovering silently over a road, but unless investigators can establish viewing conditions, angular size, duration and possible conventional explanations, the description remains a claim rather than verified evidence.

## Using dates, shapes and places without overclaiming

The most responsible way to use the New Hampshire NUFORC archive is to look for patterns while avoiding assumptions about causes.

### Dates can reveal shared events

If multiple reports appear from the same evening across different towns, investigators can compare them with known meteor showers, satellite trains, military exercises, rocket launches or unusual weather conditions.

A cluster does not automatically imply a single unknown object. Sometimes it simply shows that many people noticed the same ordinary event from different locations. This is one reason why sighting spikes need external verification before they are treated as evidence of an anomaly. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team Report... ### Shape categories are often less precise than they appear

The shape labels can be misleading if treated too literally. A witness who reports a “triangle” may have seen three lights whose connecting structure was not actually visible. A “cylinder” may be an aircraft viewed from a particular angle. A “fireball” may be a meteor.

The categories are valuable because they help organise reports, but they do not establish that all objects with the same label share the same origin. When used carefully, the NUFORC archive helps answer several worthwhile questions about modern New Hampshire UFO reporting:
