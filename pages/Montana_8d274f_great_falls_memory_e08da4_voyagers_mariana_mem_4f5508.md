## Introduction

[Great Falls]({{ 'great-falls/' | relative_url }}) did something unusual with its most famous UFO story: it turned it into a sports brand. Rather than treating the 1950 [Mariana film]({{ 'mariana-film/' | relative_url }}) as a strange historical footnote, the city gradually folded it into a public identity that mixed local humour, civic pride and lingering mystery. The result was the Great Falls Voyagers baseball team, a name and visual identity that openly referenced one of Montana’s best-known UFO cases while still leaving room for scepticism about what was actually filmed. The branding matters because it shows how an unresolved sighting can become local memory. In Great Falls, the UFO story survived not because everyone agreed on what happened, but because the ambiguity itself became part of the city’s character. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sportslogos.net/2015/09/05/truly-trippy-the-story-behind-the-great-falls-voyagers/baseball/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sportslogos.net">[SportsLogos.Net News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sportslogos.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NewsTruly Trippy: The Story Behind the Great Falls VoyagersSeptember 5, 2015 — 5 Sept 2015 — The so-called Mariana UFO Incident is the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2015</span></span></span>

## How the Mariana film entered ballpark identity

When the local baseball club adopted the name “Voyagers” in 2008, it was not choosing a random science-fiction theme. The rebrand deliberately connected the team to the Mariana UFO incident, which had occurred at the city’s baseball grounds in 1950 when team manager Nick Mariana filmed two bright objects crossing the sky. The connection between the sighting and local baseball already existed in public memory because Mariana was part of the baseball organisation when the event happened. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_UFO_film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mariana UFO film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mariana UFO film</span></span></span>

The club’s new identity made that connection visible. Team logos incorporated flying-saucer imagery and extraterrestrial references, while the mascot Orbit turned the UFO theme into family entertainment rather than a claim that alien visitors had definitely arrived in [Montana]({{ 'why-montana-became-a-ufo-landmark/' | relative_url }}). Local reporting and team statements have repeatedly acknowledged that the branding grew from the Mariana story and from Great Falls’ broader reputation as a place associated with unusual sightings. Moving to “Voyagers” gave the franchise a locally distinctive identity that could not be mistaken for a copy of another club. The UFO connection provided a story that belonged specifically to Great Falls. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sportslogos.net/2015/09/05/truly-trippy-the-story-behind-the-great-falls-voyagers/baseball/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sportslogos.net">[SportsLogos.Net News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sportslogos.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NewsTruly Trippy: The Story Behind the Great Falls VoyagersSeptember 5, 2015 — 5 Sept 2015 — The so-called Mariana UFO Incident is the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2015</span></span></span>

## Lewis and Clark exploration versus alien imagery

One reason the branding endured is that “Voyagers” works on two different levels.

At first glance, the name evokes exploration. Great Falls occupies an important place in the history of the Lewis and Clark expedition, whose members had to navigate the difficult portage around the Missouri River falls. The idea of voyagers therefore fits established regional history and western heritage. At the same time, the word can suggest travellers from elsewhere, allowing the club to reference UFO folklore without explicitly naming aliens in the team title. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sportslogos.net/2015/09/05/truly-trippy-the-story-behind-the-great-falls-voyagers/baseball/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sportslogos.net">[SportsLogos.Net News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sportslogos.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NewsTruly Trippy: The Story Behind the Great Falls VoyagersSeptember 5, 2015 — 5 Sept 2015 — The so-called Mariana UFO Incident is the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2015</span></span></span>

The logo system made that double meaning clearer. Alien faces, flying saucers and the mascot Orbit pushed the visual identity toward science fiction, while the team name itself remained broad enough to connect with older exploration narratives. Sports branding analysts have pointed to this combination as one reason the rebrand felt more substantial than a novelty gimmick. The UFO imagery was memorable, but it was attached to themes of travel, discovery and local history that already existed in Great Falls culture. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sportslogos.net/2015/09/05/truly-trippy-the-story-behind-the-great-falls-voyagers/baseball/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sportslogos.net">[SportsLogos.Net News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sportslogos.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NewsTruly Trippy: The Story Behind the Great Falls VoyagersSeptember 5, 2015 — 5 Sept 2015 — The so-called Mariana UFO Incident is the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2015</span></span></span>

That balance also helped avoid a problem faced by some UFO-themed attractions and businesses. Great Falls did not need to claim that extraterrestrials had unquestionably visited the city. The branding could celebrate the story while leaving the underlying case unresolved.

## Why the city could embrace a disputed case

The Mariana film remains debated. Investigators have variously suggested [aircraft reflections]({{ 'jet-reflections/' | relative_url }}), military jets and other conventional [explanations]({{ 'explanations-34f983/' | relative_url }}), while supporters of the case argue that none fully accounts for the filmed objects. Official reviews never produced a universally accepted conclusion, and the film continues to appear in discussions of classic UFO evidence. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Falls_Voyagers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Great Falls Voyagers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Great Falls Voyagers</span></span></span>

Paradoxically, that uncertainty made the story easier to adopt as local folklore.

If the case had been conclusively exposed as a hoax, it would have lost much of its appeal. If it had somehow been officially confirmed as extraterrestrial, it would have moved into a very different category of historical significance. Instead, Great Falls inherited a story that sits in the middle: famous enough to attract attention, uncertain enough to remain discussable, and closely tied to a familiar local setting. The baseball connection transformed a disputed UFO report into something residents could reference casually without taking a position on what Mariana actually filmed. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_%281956_film%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO (1956 film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO (1956 film</span></span></span>

The city’s museum and historical organisations have generally treated the incident in a similar way. The film is preserved as part of local history, and the event is discussed because it happened and influenced Great Falls, not because historians claim to know exactly what appeared in the sky. That distinction is important. The memory being preserved is the social impact of the sighting as much as the sighting itself. <span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.greatfallshistorymuseum.org/blog/the-mariana-ufo-great-falls-visiting-voyagers" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: greatfallshistorymuseum.org">[The History Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">greatfallshistorymuseum.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The History MuseumMariana UFO Incident: Great Falls&#x27; Visiting Voyagers1 Sept 2025 — Great Falls has had over 100 reported UFO sightings...</span></span></span>

## What the branding says about local belief and doubt

The Voyagers identity reveals something important about UFO culture in Montana. Public memory does not always depend on public belief.

Many residents who enjoy the team’s alien imagery may remain unconvinced that Mariana filmed anything extraordinary. Others see the incident as one of the strongest classic UFO cases. The branding works because it does not require agreement. Fans can treat the story as a mystery, a local legend, a historical curiosity or a genuine unexplained event. The team identity accommodates all of those interpretations simultaneously. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://news.sportslogos.net/2015/09/05/truly-trippy-the-story-behind-the-great-falls-voyagers/baseball/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: news.sportslogos.net">[SportsLogos.Net News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">news.sportslogos.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NewsTruly Trippy: The Story Behind the Great Falls VoyagersSeptember 5, 2015 — 5 Sept 2015 — The so-called Mariana UFO Incident is the mo...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 5, 2015</span></span></span>

That flexibility helps explain why the Mariana incident has lasted longer in [Great Falls memory]({{ 'great-falls/' | relative_url }}) than many other reported sightings. Most UFO reports remain confined to archives, specialist books or occasional documentaries. The Mariana story became attached to a ballpark, a mascot, merchandise and a team name seen repeatedly by residents and visitors. Through baseball, a contested piece of UFO history became part of everyday civic culture. <span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariana_UFO_film" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mariana UFO film</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mariana UFO film</span></span></span>
